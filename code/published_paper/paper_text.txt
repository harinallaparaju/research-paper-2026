Fingerprint  authentication  is widely employed  for identity verification  due to its reliance  on unique biometric  
traits, such as ridge patterns  and minutiae  points. Despite its advantages,  traditional  fingerprint  systems 
remain susceptible  to spoofing  and privacy breaches,  raising significant  security  concerns.  To mitigate  these 
risks, cancelable  biometrics  have emerged  as a viable solution  by irreversibly  transforming  biometric  features,  
thereby preventing  the reconstruction  of the original fingerprint  even in the event of template  compromise.  
This paper proposes  a novel approach  for generating  cancelable  biometric  templates  from fingerprint  images 
by transforming  minutiae  points and securely  mapping  them onto elliptic curves. Authentication  is performed  
entirely within the transformed  domain using a fuzzy vault scheme,  thus preserving  template  security  
throughout  the matching  process.  The efficacy of the suggested  method is demonstrated  by experimental  
evaluations  on the FVC2002  and FVC2004  datasets,  which reveal EER values (in %) of 0.0, 0.02, and 5.0 
for DB1, DB2, and DB3 of FVC2002  and 2.4 for DB1 of FVC2004,  respectively.  The security  and comparative  
analysis  prove the effectiveness  of the proposed  method over current methods.
1. Introduction
Modern  security  technologies  have made great strides in biometric  
authentication  systems  (BAS), which provide  a reliable  substitute  for 
traditional  knowledge-based  (passwords,  for example)  or token-based  
(ID cards, for example)  authentication  techniques.  BAS offers improved  
identity  verification  with increased  resistance  against  prevalent  threats 
like credential  theft and impersonation  by utilizing  distinctive  physi-
ological  or behavioral  traits, such as fingerprints,  iris patterns,  palm-
prints, or facial features.  At the core of these systems  lies the biometric  
template:  a compact  and discriminative  digital representation  of an 
individual’s  biometric  traits, which serves as the basis for comparison  
during the authentication  process  (see Fig.  1).
Despite  their advantages,  the deployment  of BAS introduces  serious 
security  and privacy  concerns,  particularly  regarding  the protection  
of stored biometric  templates.  Various  attack strategies  have shown 
that biometric  templates,  if compromised,  can be reverse-engineered  
to reconstruct  original  biometric  traits, leading  to unauthorized  access 
and irreversible  loss of biometric  confidentiality  [1,2]. Among  the 
most prominent  attacks are template  inversion  attacks,  where adver-
saries attempt  to reconstruct  biometric  traits from stored templates;

Cancelable  biometric  techniques  have become  a viable way to 
solve privacy  and security  issues in biometric  systems.  In cancelable  
biometrics,  the original  biometric  data is subjected  to a non-invertible  
transformation,  generating  an application-specific  pseudo-identity  for 
storage  and matching.  As defined  by the ISO/IEC  24745:2011  standard,  
two essential  properties  must be upheld:  irreversibility  and cancelabil-
ity [3]. Even if the transformation  parameters  are revealed,  irreversibil-
ity guarantees  that it is computationally  impossible  to recreate  the 
original  biometric  data from the altered template.  Cancelability  allows 
for the generation  of multiple,  distinct  templates  from the same biomet-
ric source,  facilitating  revocation  and preventing  cross-matching  across 
different  applications.  Preserving  high recognition  accuracy  within the 
transformed  domain  is crucial to maintaining  system usability  while 
ensuring  robust template  protection.

cancelable  fingerprint  templates  by integrating  elliptic curve crypto-
graphic  transformations  of minutiae  features  with a Fuzzy Vault-based  
authentication  framework.  The use of elliptic curve cryptography  (ECC) 
offers strong security  guarantees  with efficient  computation,  while the 
Fuzzy Vault scheme  provides  robust authentication  even with intra-
class variability  and prevents  unauthorized  access. The combination  
of ECC and fuzzy vault systems  provides  a powerful  and efficient  
solution  for biometric  authentication  security.  ECC provides  robust 
cryptographic  security  with smaller  key sizes than RSA, allowing  for 
faster computations  and reduced  storage  requirements,  ideal for mobile 
and IoT devices.  The fuzzy vault approach  improves  biometric  robust-
ness by allowing  approximate  matching  while safeguarding  templates  
with chaff points, limiting  inversion  attacks,  and enabling  template  
revocation.  Combining  these technologies  results in a tiered security  
system,  with ECC protecting  cryptographic  keys and the fuzzy vault 
protecting  biometric  templates.  This technique  protects  privacy,  as-
sures scalability,  and allows for secure regeneration  if hacked.  This 
framework,  designed  for real-world  applications,  facilitates  distributed  
systems  by balancing  efficiency  and security  in resource-constrained  
contexts  while protecting  biometric  data from potential  breaches.  Its 
versatility  makes it a good fit for modern  authentication  methods.  The 
proposed  approach  aims to enhance  template  security,  ensure revo-
cability,  and maintain  high authentication  accuracy,  thus addressing  
critical challenges  in the design of next-generation  biometric  systems.

over traditional  authentication  methods,  such as passwords  and tokens.  
However,  one of the major concerns  with these systems  is the potential  
for identity  theft, as fingerprint  data cannot be changed  once compro-
mised. Furthermore,  research  has shown that fingerprint  images can be 
reconstructed  if minutiae  points are exposed.  In order to address  these challenges  and enhance  the security  of fingerprint  templates,  several 
techniques  have been proposed.
Ratha et al. [4] proposed  several constructs  for implementing  can-
cellable  biometrics  in fingerprint  recognition,  focusing  on feature do-
main transformations.  Their work demonstrated  the possibility  of gen-
erating  a large number  of cancellable  fingerprint  templates  with min-
imal loss in recognition  accuracy,  thereby  increasing  the security  of 
biometric  data.
Gao et al. [5] facial features  extracted  by deep neural networks  
contain  personally  identifiable  information  that can even be recreated  
from facial photos.  This calls for strong biometric  template  protec-
tion (BTP) systems  that adhere to requirements  including  accuracy  
preservation,  revocability,  unlinkability,  and non-invertibility.  In order 
to produce  stable and cancelable  face templates,  the authors  present  
a novel approach  that combines  Simhash  and Multiple  Partial Walsh 
Transformations  (MPWT).
Sandhya  and Prasad [6] proposed  a method  for constructing  can-
celable fingerprint  templates  by integrating  bit strings derived  from 
both local and global attributes  of minutiae  points. In a related line 
of work, Ali et al. [7] introduced  a rotation-invariant,  non-invertible  
transformation  technique,  which was subsequently  refined  in their later 
study [8]. Furthermore,  Ali et al. [9] presented  the Polynomial  Vault 
framework,  which employs  polynomial  curves for secure key binding  
within biometric  systems.  Although  this approach  offers strong security  
guarantees,  it was observed  to perform  poorly under same-key  attack 
scenarios.
Jignesh  et al. [10] presented  an alignment-free  fingerprint  authen-
tication  technique  that uses Elliptic  Curve Signcryption  to improve  the 
system’s  computational  efficiency  and security  by combining  minu-
tiae point descriptors  with Scale-Invariant  Feature  Transform  (SIFT) 
keypoint  descriptors.
Champaneria  et al. [11] presented  a cancellable  biometric  authen-
tication  scheme  based on geometric  transformations.  Their approach  
leverages  non-reversible  transformations  to secure fingerprint  tem-
plates, protecting  user identities  from unauthorized  access. This study 
emphasizes  the advantages  of geometric  transformations  in making  
templates  resistant  to reconstruction  attacks,  thus ensuring  privacy  and 
security  in biometric  systems.
M.S.Khan  et al. [12] discusses  how important  it is to improve  
security  and privacy  in biometric  systems,  especially  for palm print 
templates.  Because  compromised  biometric  data is irreplaceable  and

feature vector, thereby  ensuring  non-invertibility  and eliminating  the 
need to store any original  biometric  data.
The fuzzy commitment  scheme,  proposed  by Juels and Watten-
berg [17], integrates  error-correcting  codes with cryptographic  prim-
itives to enhance  the security  of biometric  templates.  In this scheme,  
the binary representation  of a user’s biometric  data is combined  with 
a codeword  from an error correction  code using a bitwise  exclusive-
OR (XOR) operation,  and the resulting  value is stored as the protected  
template.  During authentication,  a new biometric  sample is similarly  
binarized  and XORed with the stored data to recover  the original  
codeword,  provided  the input is sufficiently  close to the original.  This 
enables  both secure key retrieval  and reliable  identity  verification,  even 
in the presence  of minor biometric  variations.
The fuzzy vault scheme,  introduced  by Juels and Sudan [18], se-
cures a secret key by binding  it with a set of genuine  biometric  data 
points – such as fingerprint  minutiae  – augmented  with a large number  
of randomly  generated  chaff points. These chaff points obscure  the gen-
uine data, rendering  the vault unintelligible  to unauthorized  users. Dur-
ing authentication,  if the presented  biometric  data sufficiently  overlaps  
with the original  feature set, the system can successfully  reconstruct  
the key and verify the user’s identity,  thereby  enabling  secure and 
error-tolerant  biometric  authentication.
Nandakumar  [19] proposed  a fuzzy commitment-based  cryptosys-
tem in which a mathematical  function  representing  minutiae  informa-
tion is used to compute  the binary representation  of fingerprint  data. 
The approach  involves  quantizing  the Fourier  spectrum  of this function  
to generate  a robust binary string, which is subsequently  secured  using 
the fuzzy commitment  scheme.  To address  alignment  issues, high-
curvature  points are utilized  for aligning  the probe fingerprint,  and the 
corresponding  alignment  data is stored as helper information.  While 
this technique  enhances  matching  accuracy,  the storage  of auxiliary  
data introduces  potential  vulnerabilities,  as it may be exploited  in 
certain attack scenarios.
Li et al. [20] introduced  a method  for deriving  the binary rep-
resentation  of fingerprint  features,  which are subsequently  protected  
using the fuzzy commitment  scheme.  The binary string is generated  
from minutiae  triplets,  where each triplet consists  of three minutiae  
points that meet a specific  threshold  criterion,  such as the area of 
the triangle  formed  by the points. While this approach  offers strong 
security,  it necessitates  multiple  fingerprint  samples  and involves  a 
complex  procedure  for generating  the binary template,  which could be considered  a limitation  in terms of both efficiency  and practical  
implementation.
Joni Saputra  et al. [21] proposed  a distance-based  fuzzy vault 
method  to enhance  the accuracy  of biometric  authentication  systems.  
Their approach  incorporates  several modifications,  including  minutiae  
filtering  and candidate  point identification  techniques,  which eliminate  
the need for image alignment  and rotation  during both registration  
and authentication.  Additionally,  the distance-based  method  avoids the 
generation  of auxiliary  helper data, which could otherwise  pose a risk 
of information  leakage,  thereby  reducing  the potential  for exploits  by 
impostors.
Vivek Singh et al. [22] proposed  a method  for filtering  genuine  
vault points from a combined  set of genuine  and chaff points, thereby  
enhancing  the security  of stored fingerprint  templates.  This technique  
ensures  that only genuine  minutiae  contribute  to the key recovery  
process,  reducing  the risk of unauthorized  access. Additionally,  to 
address  minutiae  misalignment  during verification,  they introduced  a 
Principal  Component  Analysis  (PCA)-based  alignment  technique,  which 
significantly  improves  the accuracy  of the verification  process.
In [23], Nandakumar  introduced  a fuzzy vault technique  that uti-
lizes transformed  minutiae  point information,  enabling  revocability  of 
the biometric  template.  This transformation  is password-based,  and 
a Cyclic Redundancy  Check (CRC) is employed  to detect errors in 
the generated  key during the decoding  process  in authentication.  This 
approach  enhances  both the security  and reliability  of the system by 
ensuring  key integrity.
Li and Hu [24] proposed  an alignment-free  technique  for securing  
fingerprint  templates  using the fuzzy vault method.  They utilized  pair-
polar structures  of minutiae  points as features  and constructed  separate  
vaults for each pair-polar  structure.  While this approach  enhances  
security,  the need to generate  multiple  vaults introduces  additional  
complexity  to the encoding  and decoding  processes.
Uludag  and Jain [25] introduced  a fuzzy vault method  to secure 
fingerprint  templates  by replacing  traditional  error correction  codes 
with a Cyclic Redundancy  Check (CRC). During enrollment,  minutiae  
locations  are mapped  onto a polynomial  derived  from a secret key, and 
polynomial  reconstruction  is achieved  through  Lagrange  interpolation  
during authentication.  In a subsequent  extension  of this work, Nan-
dakumar  et al. [26] enhanced  the vault by incorporating  orientation  
values alongside  minutiae  locations  during encoding.  Additionally,  they 
implemented  a coarse filtering  technique  to selectively  retain genuine  
minutiae  during the decoding  process.
Rathgeb  et al. [27] proposed  a multi-biometric  fuzzy vault tech-
nique that integrates  fingerprint  and facial recognition  features.  By 
combining  multiple  biometric  modalities  at the feature level before 
applying  the fuzzy vault scheme,  this approach  enhances  both the 
security  and accuracy  of the biometric  system.  The study demonstrates  
notable  improvements  in recognition  accuracy  and resistance  to spoof-
ing attacks by leveraging  the complementary  strengths  of multiple  
biometric  traits.
Tams et al. [28] proposed  a method  to defend against  cross-
matching  attacks by arranging  minutiae  points and chaff points in a 
hexagonal  grid structure.  This arrangement  complicates  attempts  to 
cross-match  with vault points from other databases,  thereby  preventing  
the retrieval  of the original  minutiae  points. Additionally,  the security  
of the minutiae-based  fuzzy vault against  various  attack vectors  is 
further analyzed  in their work.
Abdullahi  et al. [29] conducted  a comprehensive  survey on attacks 
targeting  Biometric  Authentication  Systems  (BAS), examining  various  
attack vectors,  including  inversion  attacks,  false acceptance  attacks,  
and attacks involving  record multiplicity.  Their findings  emphasize  the 
urgent need for secure and cancellable  biometric  systems  to safeguard  
the integrity  and privacy  of biometric  data. Jin et al. [30] introduce

3. Preliminary
3.1. Fuzzy vault scheme
Biometric  cryptosystems  must address  the challenge  of intra-user  
variability  in biometric  data. The Fuzzy Vault scheme,  proposed  by 
Juels and Sudan [18], is a widely adopted  method  for binding  a 
cryptographic  key with noisy biometric  data in a secure manner.  Unlike 
conventional  cryptography,  which expects  exact keys, the fuzzy vault 
allows unlocking  even when the input features  are only approximately  
correct.
3.1.1. Overview
The fuzzy vault is a form of secure sketch that hides a polynomial  
corresponding  to a secret (e.g., a cryptographic  key) among a large set 
of random  chaff points. Only someone  with a sufficiently  overlapping  
set of biometric  features  can recover  enough  genuine  points to recon-
struct the polynomial  and retrieve  the secret. It leverages  the hardness  
of the polynomial  reconstruction  problem  over noisy data, ensuring  
security  even if the vault is exposed.
Formally,  let 𝑆 be a secret, and let 𝑄= {𝑞1, 𝑞2,…, 𝑞𝑡} be the user’s 
biometric  features  extracted  from a fingerprint  image. A polynomial  
𝐹(𝑥) of degree 𝑘 is generated  from 𝑆, and each 𝑞𝑖∈𝑄 is evaluated  
under 𝐹 to produce  (𝑞𝑖, 𝐹(𝑞𝑖)). These are the genuine  points. To hide 
these, a number  of chaff points (𝑥𝑗, 𝑦𝑗) are added such that 𝑥𝑗∉𝑄 and 
𝑦𝑗≠𝐹(𝑞𝑗).
The final vault 𝑉 is the union of genuine  and chaff points:
𝑉= {(𝑞𝑖, 𝐹(𝑞𝑖))} ∪ {( 𝑥𝑗, 𝑦𝑗)}

significantly  overlaps  with 𝑄 can reconstruct  𝐹(𝑥) and thus retrieve  𝑆
(see Fig.  2).
3.1.2. Phases of the Fuzzy vault
The fuzzy vault scheme  primarily  consists  of two phases:  Locking
and Unlocking . In the Locking  phase, the vault is created  by combining  
genuine  biometric  features  with a secret. During the Unlocking  phase, 
the goal is to retrieve  the secret by attempting  to open the vault using 
a query template.

3.1.3. Security  considerations
The security  of the fuzzy vault lies in the difficulty  of distinguishing  
genuine  points from chaff points without  substantial  overlap  between  
the feature sets. Without  a query set that closely resembles  the real 
biometric  traits, an attacker  cannot rebuild  the polynomial,  even if 
they manage  to get into the vault. The presence  of numerous  decoy 
points makes it computationally  infeasible  for the attacker  to derive 
the original  data.
3.2. Elliptic Curve Cryptography  (ECC)
Elliptic  Curve Cryptography  (ECC) is a public-key  cryptosystem  that 
provides  high security  with significantly  smaller  key sizes compared  
to traditional  systems  like RSA [31,32]. ECC operates  over the alge-
braic structure  of elliptic curves defined  on finite fields. Its efficiency  
makes ECC particularly  well-suited  for resource-constrained  environ-

3.2.1. Mapping  integers  to curve points
In many ECC-based  applications,  including  biometric  systems,  we 
need to bind integer values (e.g., hashed features,  keys, etc.) to elliptic 
curve points. Since ECC operates  on points, a deterministic  mapping  
from an integer to a valid point on the curve is essential.
In elliptic curve-based  cryptographic  systems,  it is often necessary  
to represent  integer values as points on the curve. A simple and secure 
approach  is to use scalar multiplication  of the base point 𝐺 with the 
integer 𝑥:
𝑃=𝑥.𝐺
Here, 𝑥 is the input integer and 𝐺 is the generator  point defined  on 
the elliptic curve. The result 𝑃 is a valid point on the curve.
This representation  is deterministic  and produces  a unique point for 
every integer modulo  the order of 𝐺. Since scalar multiplication  is a 
core operation  in ECC and is computationally  hard to reverse  (due to 
the Elliptic  Curve Discrete  Logarithm  Problem),  it offers a secure and 
efficient  way to bind an integer to a curve point [33].
More complex  mappings  like the Shallue–van  de Woestijne  (SWU) 
algorithm  or Elligator  offer uniform  and privacy-preserving  mappings  
but require  more computational  resources  [34].4. Proposed  method

5. Experiment  results
A number  of performance  indicators,  such as Equal Error Rate 
(EER) and Receiver  Operating  Characteristic  (ROC) curves,  are used to 
thoroughly  assess the performance  of the suggested  method.  We used 
datasets  from the Fingerprint  Verification  Challenge  (FVC) 2002 and 
FVC 2004 to evaluate  the system’s  performance.  In particular,  one FVC 
2004 dataset,  DB1, and three FVC 2002 datasets,  DB1, DB2, and DB3, 
were employed.  Each dataset  contains  800 photos total, consisting  of 
grayscale  fingerprint  images from 100 participants,  each of whom has 
eight images.
The proposed  method  incorporates  two critical tuning parameters  
to enhance  both robustness  and security.  The first parameter  is the
quantization  factor (𝑓), which is employed  to quantize  the minutiae  
descriptor  values (such as position  and orientation).  This quantization  
helps mitigate  intra-class  variations , natural  variations  in fingerprint  
impressions  from the same user. The second parameter  is the degree
(𝑘) of the polynomial  𝐹(𝑥), which directly  determines  the minimum  
number  of genuine  points required  to reconstruct  the polynomial  and 
successfully  recover  the secret 𝑠 from the vault. By adjusting  𝑘, the 
system can control  its tolerance  to inter-class  similarities , accidental  
similarities  between  different  users, thereby  enhancing  the overall 
discriminative  power and security  of the vault. A higher polynomial  
degree increases  the minimum  number  of correct matches  required,  
thus reducing  the chances  of false acceptances.

6. Security  analysis
A thorough  security  assessment  of the suggested  approach  is pro-
vided in this section,  with special attention  paid to three crucial char-
acteristics:  revocability,  non-invertibility,  and unlinkability.
6.1. Threat model
We use a formal threat model to give a systematic  evaluation  of our 
suggested  system’s  security.  This model uses the framework  developed  
by Ratha et al. [40], which identifies  eight critical points of attack in 
a biometric  system,  to define the adversary’s  capabilities  and analyze  
potential  vulnerabilities  (see Fig.  6).
Adversary  capabilities
The following  capabilities  are assumed  for a strong and realistic  
adversary:
•The attacker  can read, replay, alter, and intercept  any data sent 
over the communication  channel  between  the client (user’s de-
vice) and the server (database).  It is a common  man-in-the-middle
assumption.
•The attacker  can access the server-side  database  in full read mode. 
Thus, they are able to take all of the users’ fuzzy vaults (𝑉) and 
associated  public keys (𝑃 𝑢𝑏).•The attacker  is aware of every detail of the proposed  system’s  
architecture,  including  the fuzzy vault construction  algorithm,  
quantization  method,  feature extraction  procedure,  and ECC pa-
rameters.  This follows  Kerckhoffs’s  Principle , where security  
relies on the secrecy  of the key/biometric  rather than obscurity  
of the algorithm.
Adversary  limitations
The adversary  is assumed  to be unable to:
•Physically  access or compromise  the client device.  Therefore,  they 
cannot steal the secret key (𝑠) from the client’s memory  or install 
a Trojan horse on the feature extraction  module.
•Obtain the user’s actual biometric  trait (e.g., fingerprint)  to con-
duct a spoofing  attack. While spoofing  is a real threat, defenses  
such as liveness  detection  are considered  outside  the scope of this 
template  protection  scheme.
We assess our system’s  resilience  against  the eight attack points 
identified  by Ratha et al. [40] using this threat model:
1.Sensor  Attack (Spoofing):  An attacker  presents  a fake finger-
print to the sensor. Our scheme,  which focuses  on template  
protection,  does not inherently  defend against  this. This vulner-
ability is typically  addressed  by liveness  detection  mechanisms  
at the hardware  or sensor level. In our proposed  method,  the 
attacks performed  on the data at sensor point are not protected  
as they are hardware  oriented.
2.Channel  between  Sensor  and Feature  Extractor:  An attacker  
might try to replay raw fingerprint  data that has already  been 
intercepted.  According  to the constraints  of our model, this 
attack takes place on the client device,  which we presume  to 
be secure.
3.Feature  Extractor  Attack (Trojan  Horse):  An attacker  could 
compromise  the feature extraction  module  to output a prede-
termined  set of minutiae.  This is also a client-side  attack and 
is outside  the scope of our threat model. We do not consider  
this attack in our proposed  method  as we assume  that the client 
software  is secure.
4.Channel  between  Feature  Extractor  and Vault Creation:  Be-
fore the vault is built, our system performs  feature extraction,  
quantization,  ECC mapping,  and polynomial  projection  on the 
client device.  There is no transmission  of raw or unprotected  
feature data. Consequently,  because  the sensitive  operations  are 
localized,  this channel  is not vulnerable  in the traditional  sense.
5.Matcher  Attack (Fuzzy Vault Unlocking):  The vault unlocking  
mechanism  is the ‘‘matcher’’  in our system.  In order to track the 
changes  in matching  score, an attacker  could try a hill-climbing  
attack by repeatedly  altering  a synthetic  template.  Due to the 
fuzzy vault scheme’s  lack of a similarity  score, our system is 
resistant  to this. Unlocking  has a binary outcome:  either the 
polynomial  is successfully  reconstructed  (Success)  or it is not 
(Failure).  The attacker  is unable to cleverly  evolve a template  
due to the absence  of immediate  feedback.
6.Stored Template  Attack (Database  Breach):  If an attacker  
breaches  the database,  they acquire  the fuzzy vault 𝑉 and the 
public key 𝑃 𝑢𝑏. The security  relies on two principles:Journal  of Information  Security  and Applications  94 (2025)  104243

6.2. Unlinkability
The feature that guarantees  a biometric  template  cannot be linked 
to a person’s  true identity  or correlated  with templates  kept in other 
applications  or systems  is known as unlinkability  in biometric  systems.  
This feature ensures  that even if a template  is compromised  in one 
situation,  it cannot be used to connect  to biometric  records  or betray 
the identity  of the user in another.  In order to protect  user privacy  
and prevent  unwanted  tracking,  profiling,  or cross-matching  between  
databases,  unlinkability  is crucial.
The proposed  method  guarantees  that templates  created  using iden-
tical fingerprint  biometric  information  cannot be connected  to other 
systems  or applications.  For authentication,  the templates  are stored

elliptic curve with a designated  base point G. Consequently,  under 
the proposed  system,  the templates  stored in different  systems  remain 
unlinkable,  as detailed  below:

quantitative  proof that our suggested  approach  attains a high level 
of unlinkability,  rendering  it resistant  to privacy  and cross-matching

templates  from different  systems,  will not be able to cross-match  these 
templates  or reconstruct  the original  biometric  data, as the mapping  of 
the data onto the elliptic curve is fundamentally  altered by the choice 
of parameters.
6.3. Non-invertibility
In cancelable  biometrics,  non-invertibility  means that reconstruct-
ing a person’s  original  biometric  information  from a stored template  
or its encrypted  representation  is computationally  infeasible.  This fea-
ture guarantees  that no significant  information  about the underlying  
biometric  characteristics  will be revealed  by the exposed  biometric  
template,  even in the case of a data breach.  The template  must be 
created  and saved in a way that makes it impossible  for adversaries  to 
reverse-engineer  the original  biometric  data, even if they have complete  
access to the stored data, in order to ensure non-invertibility.In the proposed  method,  non-invertibility  is enforced  using Elliptic  
Curve Cryptography  (ECC), specifically  with the brainpoolP256r1  curve 
from the TinyEC  library.  After feature extraction  and binarization,  the 
fingerprint  template  is cryptographically  bound to a point on the ellip-
tic curve using a 256-bit  private key. This binding  acts as a one-way,  
irreversible  transformation  without  knowledge  of the private key. The
brainpoolP256r1  curve offers a high-security  level, conforming  to 
standards  set by the German  Federal  Office for Information  Security  
(BSI). A 256-bit  key on this curve provides  approximately  128 bits of 
security , which translates  to an estimated  2128 operations  needed  to 
derive the private key from a public ECC point.
Even under ideal attack conditions,  a brute-force  attempt  to recover  
the private key used in the ECC-based  transformation  would require  
approximately  3.4 × 1038 operations.  Assuming  an optimistic  scenario  
where an attacker  possesses  a machine  capable  of performing  1012
operations  per second (1 trillion ops/s), it would still take more than 
1018 years to exhaust  the key space—an  amount  of time that vastly 
exceeds  the age of the universe.
Additionally,  the non-existence  of sub-exponential  time algorithms  
for solving  the Elliptic  Curve Discrete  Logarithm  Problem  (ECDLP)  on 
prime fields provides  strong theoretical  assurance.  In contrast  to RSA 
or some symmetric  encryption  schemes  that have known structural  
weaknesses  or partial vulnerabilities,  ECC remains  robust and secure 
under all currently  known cryptanalytic  techniques.
The transformation  of binary fingerprint  features  into elliptic curve 
points is inherently  non-invertible.  During the process,  the original  
fingerprint  features  are irreversibly  lost due to binarization  and their 
binding  to elliptic curve points using ECC operations.  Without  knowl-
edge of the private key used in this binding,  there is no mathematically  
feasible  method  to reverse  the transformation  and recover  the original  
features.  Furthermore,  even partial information  leakage  is effectively  
prevented,  as there exists no deterministic  or direct mapping  between  
the protected  template  and the original  biometric  input. This ensures  
strong resistance  against  reconstruction  or inversion  attacks.  The hill-
climbing  attack is based on score evolution  through  the use of several 
reference  templates.  As a result, the hill-climbing  attack cannot be 
performed  on our proposed  technique  as the attacker  lacks the nec-
essary feedback  information  to construct  an original  template.  In an
Attack via Record  Multiplicity , the attacker  attempts  to reconstruct  
the original  biometric  template  by correlating  multiple  instances  of its 
transformed  versions.  However,  in our proposed  method,  even with 
access to several transformed  templates,  the attacker  is unable to 
recover  the original  biometric  data due to the strong non-invertible  us-
ing Elliptic  Curve Cryptography,  specifically  with the brainpoolP256r1  
curve and the fuzzy vault encoding.
6.4. Revocability
A crucial feature of biometric  template  protection  systems  is re-
vocability,  which guarantees  that biometric  data can be successfully  
replaced  or revoked  without  forcing  the user to re-enroll  in the event 
of a security  breach or template  compromise.  This ensures  that the 
security  and privacy  of users are maintained,  even when a template  
is exposed  to an attacker.  In the context  of fingerprint  template  pro-
tection,  revocability  ensures  that compromised  templates  cannot be 
misused  and provides  users with a mechanism  to securely  update their 
biometric  data when necessary.
In the proposed  method,  revocability  is achieved  through  the use 
of Elliptic  Curve Cryptography  (ECC) , specifically  with the brain-
poolP256r1  curve. The fingerprint  template  is bound to a 256-bit  
ECC private key, and the transformation  from fingerprint  features  to 
cryptographic  bindings  ensures  both non-invertibility  and the ability 
to update the key when necessary.  By generating  a unique private key 
per user, the system allows for the revocation  of a compromised  tem-
plate by simply replacing  the key, without  requiring  the re-enrollment

Mechanism  of revocation
1. Key Update : If a user’s template  is compromised  or if the user

system allows for the regeneration  of a new template  using the latest 
fingerprint  features,  which is then bound to a new private key.Even  if 
an attacker  manages  to obtain an old template,  the new key ensures  
that they cannot exploit it to obtain unauthorized  access to the system.
Security  and practical  considerations
The revocability  feature greatly reduces  the potential  damage
caused by a compromised  template.  Even if an attacker  gains access to 
one template,  they cannot reuse it without  the corresponding  private 
key, which remains  secure. The use of a 256-bit  ECC private  key
provides  significant  security,  as the computational  effort required  to 
break such a key through  brute-force  or other cryptographic  attacks 
is infeasible  with current  technology.  This ensures  that the revocation  
process  is secure and the templates  remain protected  even in the event 
of a breach.  By enabling  template  updates  through  key changes,  the 
system minimizes  the risk associated  with user re-enrollment,  while still 
maintaining  high security  and privacy  standards.

6.6. Cross-matching  attack
A cross-matching  attack occurs when an attacker  tries to leverage  
multiple  biometric  templates  stored in different  systems  for the same 
user and attempts  to reconstruct  the original  biometric  data. The at-
tacker gains access to templates  stored in different  vaults, such as in 
a healthcare  system or a bank, and attempts  to cross-reference  the 
templates  to extract the genuine  biometric  data.
In the proposed  method,  each system may use different  parame-
ters such as elliptic curves,  quantization  factors,  and base points for 
template  generation  and encryption.  These parameters  directly  affect 
the transformation  of biometric  data into cryptographically  protected  
templates.  For instance,  varying  elliptic curves or base points ensure 
that even identical  biometric  data, when processed  in different  systems,  
results in different  cryptographic  representations.  Similarly,  by using 
different  quantization  factors,  the concatenated  minutiae  descriptor  
values will be different  for different  systems,  and after elliptic curve 
mapping,  a completely  different  set of points will be generated  for the 
same biometric  data.
As a result, even if an attacker  acquires  multiple  templates  from 
different  systems,  the use of distinct  parameters  makes it infeasible  
to cross-match  these templates  and guess he genuine  points or recon-
struct the original  biometric  data. The differences  in the cryptographic  
transformations  ensure that the templates  remain secure, protecting  
the genuine  points and preventing  the attacker  from successfully  per-
forming  a cross-matching  attack. Consequently,  each system generates  
a unique and secure template  that cannot be used in combination  with 
others to compromise  the user’s biometric  data.
7. Conclusion  and future work
By combining  the fuzzy vault method  and ECC-based  mapping,  
the proposed  solution  creates a strong foundation  for fingerprint-based  
biometric  identification.  Important  security  features,  including  non-
invertibility,  revocability,  and unlinkability,  are guaranteed  by this 
integration.  The approach’s  efficacy  is confirmed  by experimental  as-
sessments  on the FVC2002  database,  which show low EER and strong 
discriminability  between  genuine  and fake samples.
In order to create a safe multi-modal  recognition  system,  future 
research  will try to expand  the suggested  method  by combining  fin-
gerprint  characteristics  with other biometric  modalities.  Combining  
fingerprint  information  with other characteristics,  such iris or facial 
recognition,  could significantly  increase  the authentication  process’s  
overall security,  resilience,  and dependability.  This multi-modal  ap-
proach enables  the system to capitalize  on the strengths  of each modal-
ity while mitigating  their individual  limitations,  resulting  in a more re-
silient and versatile  authentication  framework.  Additionally,  future re-
search will explore  the integration  of this biometric  system with emerg-
ing technologies  such as blockchain  to enhance  data integrity,  ensure 
tamper-proof  storage,  and provide  robust access control  mechanisms.
