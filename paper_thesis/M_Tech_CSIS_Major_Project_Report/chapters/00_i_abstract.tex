\chapter*{\center \Large Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Biometric template protection is critical for securing identity systems against privacy breaches, with the fuzzy vault serving as a widely studied cryptographic primitive. However, traditional unimodal vaults struggle with noisy data. While multimodal fusion mitigates this, existing multimodal cryptosystems structurally penalize genuine users by treating the secondary biometric as a mandatory gate for decoding. Furthermore, centralized storage of these vaults leaves them highly susceptible to offline brute-force attacks. To address these vulnerabilities, this paper proposes a novel, decentralized multimodal biometric authentication framework implemented in two phases. In the first phase, we propose the Iris-Boosted Fuzzy Vault (IBFV). Fingerprint minutiae are securely mapped onto elliptic curves, while a stabilized iris key generates bonus polynomial points to conditionally boost the vault. If iris recovery fails, the system gracefully reverts to unimodal decoding, achieving a formally proven zero-penalty guarantee. In the second phase, to eliminate single-server trust, we propose D-IBFV, which applies (t,n)-Shamir Secret Sharing directly to the vault. The shares are distributed across an InterPlanetary File System (IPFS) network, with their cryptographic hashes anchored on a Blockchain ledger to ensure information-theoretic confidentiality and tamper-evident exact reconstruction. The efficacy of the proposed framework is demonstrated through extensive evaluations on the FVC2002, FVC2004, and CASIA-Iris-Interval datasets. IBFV achieves up to a 61.3\%relative Equal Error Rate (EER) reduction, yielding EER values (in \%) of 0.000, 0.000, 1.384, and 0.929 for FVC2002 DB1, DB2, DB3, and FVC2004 DB1, respectively. System benchmarks on EVM and Hyperledger Fabric confirm practical scalability, yielding sub-second enrollment (<806 ms) and verification (<71 ms). Furthermore, comprehensive security analysis validates that the proposed framework fully satisfies the ISO/IEC 24745 criteria for irreversibility, unlinkability, and revocability.
